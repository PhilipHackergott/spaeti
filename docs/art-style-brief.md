# Art style brief — pasted into every Codex art prompt

**Target:** a Berlin *Späti* (late-night corner kiosk) version of **Coffee Talk** — HD, hand-drawn-looking
**pixel art**. The canonical reference is `concept-art/main character target state.png` (the protagonist at a
counter): that image *is* the bar for rendering quality, pixel density, palette and mood. Match it.

**Rendering:** crisp pixel art with a consistent pixel grid (every "pixel" the same size — no mixed grids,
no soft anti-aliased edges, no smooth gradients, no vector look, no 3D render look, no photo texture).
Rich, painterly cluster shading with 4–6 tones per material, deliberate dithering only where Coffee Talk
uses it (soft light falloff), clean 1-px dark outlines on characters and props, warm rim light on faces
and hair. It should read as a skilled pixel artist's work, **not** blocky 8-bit/16-bit retro and **not**
"pixelated photo".

**Palette / light:** deep warm browns and amber lamp light inside vs. cool blue night and rain outside;
neon magenta + cyan accents (signs, fridge glow); soft grain. Bittersweet, cozy, a little melancholy.
Night-time always.

**Portrait rendering, learned from the first calibration pass (the judge scored a painterly attempt 66/100):**
skin is pale peach-pink with lifted shadows — never orange, never half-buried in black; amber lives only in a
narrow warm rim on one side, with a restrained cool-blue edge on the other. Faces are simple and clean like the
target: small readable eyes with a single highlight, thin brows, a small mouth, minimal stubble, compact short
tousled hair with the ears exposed. Surfaces are deliberate flat clusters (4–6 tones per material) with clean
one-pixel contour accents — no painterly strand-by-strand hair, no speckled noise, no soft gradient modelling.
Younger and softer beats older and brooding; neutral means "quietly present", not stern.

**Framing:** fixed side-on view from behind the counter (the player is the shopkeeper). Characters are
head-and-shoulders busts, centred, facing the viewer at the counter, slightly below eye level, like Coffee
Talk's customers. Nothing is drawn from above or in isometric.

**The set — a REAL Berlin Späti (from the user's reference photos), not a café and not a tavern:**
- **Light:** cool-white fluorescent tubes inside (a faint green-cyan cast) that spill onto the pavement; the warmth
  comes from packaging colours, the neon and the blue night outside — NOT from wood or amber lamps. Keep Coffee Talk's
  warm cluster shading, but the materials are painted plaster, laminate, cheap metal and glass.
- **Walls:** cream/white painted plaster or laminate. Directly behind the counter the signature Späti backdrop: a
  DENSE wall of cigarette packs and tobacco pouches in tight colourful rows (red / gold / blue / white generic packs,
  never real brands) on white or grey metal shelving with price-tag strips; a row of small spirits bottles; lighters,
  chewing gum and phone-cable blister packs on hooks; a small wall-mounted TV; hand-written paper price signs
  ("0,10 €"); lotto / prepaid cards; a wall clock.
- **Fridges:** tall glass-door drinks fridges with white interiors and blue/cyan LED strips, packed with generic
  beer, Mate-like, cola-like and water bottles; a sticker-covered ice-cream chest freezer.
- **Counter:** a cheap laminate counter with a WHITE cash register or a small POS terminal + card reader, stacks of
  newspapers and magazines on the front and under the glass, open boxes of chocolate bars and sweets (Kinder/duplo-
  like generic stand-ins), a jar of gummi or cables, a receipt roll, a Pfand crate of empty bottles on the floor.
- **Door / window / outside:** stickers and posters on the glass, an "OPEN" neon, a bell, roller-shutter tracks;
  outside a cream plaster facade covered in graffiti tags, a hand-painted black board with red "SPÄTI" letters (or a
  lit box sign), a faded red awning, an ATM box, a wooden bench or beer table where people sit with bottles at night,
  bikes, wet cobbles, a U-Bahn-style sign.
- **Density is the point:** every shelf full, packaging colours as the palette accents against white shelving; the
  place is cheap, bright, cluttered and alive — Coffee Talk's *rendering* on a Späti's *furniture*.
- **NOT:** dark wood panelling, beams, a wooden bar, vintage phones, jukeboxes, framed art, mugs on shelves, hanging
  plants as décor, pendant lamps, anything that reads as café, pub or tavern.

**Character design language:** strong silhouette, one signature prop or garment per character, expressive
faces (Coffee Talk proportions: realistic-ish heads, big readable eyes, subtle mouths), everyday clothes with
one saturated accent colour. The protagonist ("You", Cem's nephew) is *exactly* the man in the target image:
mid-20s, messy wavy brown hair, light stubble, red hoodie under a brown/black jacket with a red band.

**Absolutely not:** text, watermarks, signatures, borders, frames, UI mockup chrome, multiple panels,
sprite-sheet grids, isometric/top-down views, chibi/anime-big-head proportions, glossy smooth shading,
real brands or logos, photographic backgrounds.

## The tobacco wall (user reference photo, 2026-09-13 — the image itself could not be saved from chat)

A real Berlin Späti counter seen from the customer's side. A smiling shopkeeper in a dark fleece stands behind a low
white laminate counter. **Behind him, floor to ceiling, the tobacco wall:** dense rows of cigarette packs in a metal
display with a price strip along every row, a backlit brand sign at the top, columns of vape / e-cigarette boxes in
bright colours to the right with yellow starburst "Sale!" tags and hand-written prices, boxes of rolling papers and
filters, a "Blättchen nach Maß" rolling-paper stand and paper tubes on the counter, sweets (chewing gum, Mon Chéri,
Twix, M&Ms, Kinder) in the counter front, a card terminal, a small barcode scanner and a plastic cup of lighters.
Everything is packed tight, price-tagged and lit cool-white. For the game: **fake brands only** (each with its own
logo and colour, readable at pack size), real-Späti density, the same laminate/metal/fluorescent materials as the
rest of the set. The wall is a second VIEW the player turns to (docs/08).
